# Design: Claim-first assurance accounts

The accepted account should let an analyst state a Claim, a developer explain how it is realized, and a QA engineer explain how it will be challenged, without forcing all three to read source code for the upper-level argument. Azimuth then joins those explanations to emitted production artifacts and reports absent or stale support. A successful join is a participation fact, not a correctness verdict.

## One module, three explanations

A module owns `spec.md`, `design.md`, and `verification.md`; non-routine Claim review remains a distinct facet. Their headings use the same declared Claim identity. Spec owns terms, Claims and Cases. Design owns the Claim's architectural scope, relationships, optional named elements and selected controls. Verification owns the Claim's method expectations, limits and dependencies. Review records the accountable conclusion after inspecting the assembled account. Shared Sections and Elements are allowed; a Claim explicitly references shared verification Elements it requires, while Sections remain explanations.

For a new module, a change carries full Add deltas in `deltas/<module>/{spec,design,verification,judgments}.md`. The final model documents are projected before implementation and compared byte-for-byte with any authored preview. For an existing module, `Add`, `Replace` and `Remove` target stable Claim, term, section, element, mechanism or review identities; replacement and removal require an exact base fingerprint. A mismatch stops projection rather than merging prose. The change's proposal explains the problem and decision boundary, while these module facets preserve reasoning that must remain available after archive.

## Design scope and contribution

Each `## Claim design: <claim-id>` declares `Areas:`. These are the sole authored Realization Obligations for that Claim. The workspace defines Area identities and source mounts; it does not repeat the per-Claim obligation. Production `Realizes` contributions emitted from source or configuration must cover every declared Area. One contribution per Area proves only that a location participates. Review must still inspect paths, interactions, ordering, trust boundaries and failure behavior.

A Claim design may contain nested Sections, Elements and Mechanisms. A Section is an addressable explanation. An Element names a logical cooperating part such as a grant store, transaction, operation or notification path, local to its module by default. `Kind`, `Areas` and typed relationships such as `Writes` guide inspection; an Element does not demand a class with its name. A Mechanism names a selected, independently reviewable control where its identity adds value, such as atomic consumption or validated-principal forwarding. It is optional, may span several artifacts, and is not a decomposition of every implementation step. Mechanisms may list qualified Cases as optional applicability relationships; verification still owns evidence contribution to Cases. An omitted list leaves applicability unspecified, and no applicability link establishes coverage or successful enforcement. The parser checks identity shape and uniqueness; account inspection resolves each Case against the assembled specs. Section-to-Case associations remain explanatory links.

Several production sites may implement one Mechanism. Each marker retains its own compiler-qualified SourceIdentity, exact companion Artifact and source fingerprint. Assembly rejects repeated site ownership and explicit Binding mixed with marker sites. The canonical mechanism record includes the sorted collection, so adding, removing or changing a site changes review dependencies and Challenge scope. This linkage exposes participating parts; it cannot prove that every required part was tagged or that they cooperate as the prose claims.

The parser must retain explanatory prose and diagrams in the model, preserve stable heading locations, resolve Claim, Area and local-element references, and reject ambiguous duplicate identities with file and line diagnostics. The account fingerprint includes substantive design text and relationships, so changing an explanation can invalidate dependent review. The current workspace-owned obligation and mandatory per-Claim mechanism grammar cannot remain competing sources of authority in an adopted module.

## Verification scope and contribution

Each `## Claim verification: <claim-id>` may contain contextual Case verification declarations and explanatory Sections. Nested Elements are required by ownership; optional `Required elements:` references declare shared dependencies. A verification Element names a method or support component, such as an exercise, scheduled probe, reconciliation job or alert. It states what the method can and cannot establish. The required-element list is a participation floor, not a Check list or a claim of adequacy. Shared method elements are authored once and explicitly referenced by Claims. Operational methods can therefore be part of the same account as tests, with their source, queries, schedules, health rules and alert routing emitted as implementation artifacts.

Checks remain independently decidable propositions. Source/configuration extractors emit typed records identifying the Claim, existing Case contributions, verification element, artifact identity, dependencies, and fingerprint. Supporting artifacts may contribute to an element without inventing a product Check or Case. Core consumes manifests; it does not parse C#, deployment formats or alert DSLs. Validation reports a missing required element, absent Case-to-Check contribution, unresolved dependency or unsupported configuration. It never treats a tag or clean run alone as a positive conclusion. Qualifications, judgments and subject-specific execution remain separate authority and retain their own freshness rules.

## Transition and command behavior

The first adoption target is the three-module drim.dev candidate Lab gateway account. Local Azimuth must project and inspect its deltas, load the projected model, show missing production support, and report the same omissions as the temporary structural helper. No external repository path is built into framework tooling. Current Azimuth framework model uses the earlier facet grammar; migration or explicit format selection must be visible rather than silently interpreting one format as the other. The in-progress intent lifecycle owns spec projection, so this change extends it for non-spec facets without overwriting its transition rules.

Each parse or projection failure names file, line and expected syntax. CLI validation distinguishes incomplete implementation from malformed documents and from pending human review. Existing framework findings are recorded separately from regressions. No Azimuth tests are written or run for this change, per Dima's explicit instruction; product commands, compile checks and diff inspection are the permitted verification boundary.

## Open design risks

The concrete source/configuration annotation syntax and the extractor's typed manifest must be fixed before drim.dev implementation can emit support. Existing accepted Azimuth modules may need a deliberate migration to the Claim-first format; compatibility is not assumed. A generic section replacement can disrupt Claim-local hierarchy, so projection must validate its target identity and parent and compare the complete resulting document. Review dependency fingerprints must follow both authored explanation and extracted support without conflating source changes with new execution results.

Optional Mechanism Case applicability supersedes the initial no-Case-list choice. It supports both shared and Claim-local controls with one qualified identity format. Existing exact-source design fingerprints retain the authored scope. This slice does not invent a positive Judgment or qualify a verification method from that scope. Dima approved local Claim declaration names in design, verification and review facets; explicit optional Case design declarations under their Claims; and local Mechanism Case names resolved through enclosing Sections to the Claim and module. Shared or cross-Claim scope uses qualified Case references. Normalize all identities before existence checks, duplicate detection and relationship fingerprinting; retain exact source prose and guarded block fingerprints. Unknown or duplicate Case designs, invalid nesting and unqualified shared scope fail with file and line diagnostics. No compatibility layer for the earlier candidate grammar is introduced; legacy accepted facets are unchanged.

## Verification nesting revision: 2026-09-30

Dima approved optional Case verification declarations mirroring Case design, local to an enclosing Claim and normalized before duplicate/existence checks and fingerprints. Remove the redundant Sections inventory. Derive required Elements from nesting under a Claim, its Case verification or its explanatory Section; retain optional explicit Required elements lists only for shared declarations outside the Claim. Reject empty/duplicate or undeclared shared dependencies and Claim-owned references. Explanation-only Case verification needs no Element. Preserve separate extracted Element support, Check-to-Case contributions, qualifications and judgments. Guarded Case verification Add, Replace and Remove retain exact accepted-block protection. Kind remains an open descriptive identifier. No compatibility layer for the superseded candidate metadata grammar is introduced; legacy accepted verification is unaffected.

## Document-owned Check revision: 2026-09-30

Dima requested formal Checks in the ingress verification facet. The document owns Method statements, the single Terminal proposition, contribution rationale and contextual/qualified Case applicability. Source carries implementation identity only through the existing ImplementsCheck marker and exact fingerprinted repository manifest records. Optional qualified Mechanism dependencies resolve against design. Reject duplicate project-global Check authority across document, accepted legacy and source-owned candidate definitions. Preserve other currently source-owned candidate modules until deliberately migrated; never accept both semantic authorities for one Check ID. Checks need no artificial verification Element; schedule/health Elements remain independent supporting vocabulary. Declared applicability cannot discharge participation without stable extracted implementation, and participation cannot establish adequacy or evidence. Include substantive rationale and normalized cross-Claim relationships in dependent fingerprints. Guarded Check edits retain exact accepted-block protection. Accepted Evidence Binding/Qualification and Run/Challenge integration are not created by this revision.

## Claim-level Mechanism revision: 2026-10-02

Dima approved Mechanisms directly under Claim design at Markdown level three. A control that contributes to several Cases can precede those Case designs as their sibling. Local Case references resolve against the same enclosing Claim; explicit applicability, stable Mechanism identity, source linkage and fingerprint semantics remain unchanged. This does not permit Elements directly under Claims or infer relationships from order or nesting. Legacy accepted design grammar remains unchanged.

## Contextual Check Mechanism revision: 2026-10-02

Dima approved local Mechanism references in candidate Check metadata. A local name resolves against the verification document module; qualified names retain cross-module scope. Normalize before duplicate detection, existence validation and relationship fingerprinting, and reject mixed-spelling duplicates. Check identities, meaning and Case relationships are unchanged. Exact authored-source fingerprints still change with authored spelling; no qualification, evidence or assurance is inferred. Legacy accepted verification grammar remains unchanged.

## Contextual Mechanism declaration revision: 2026-10-02

Dima clarified that the Mechanism declaration itself should use a local name as well as local references. Candidate design accepts local declarations normalized against its declared module and still accepts explicitly qualified declarations for that same module. Mixed-spelling duplicates and qualified declarations belonging to another module fail at their source locations. Projection retains authored headings and guarded Parent locators; semantic identity and relationship fingerprints use normalized declarations. Exact authored-source fingerprints retain structural and spelling changes. Legacy accepted grammar is unchanged.

## Verification caption revision: 2026-10-02

Dima approved ordinary free-text Markdown captions for explanatory verification groups. Caption names have no authored identity; Markdown ancestry closes and retains structural scopes at the appropriate levels. Checks can sit one level below a caption and inherit their structural Claim and Case rather than a caption locator. Preserve substantive caption prose in the owning explanation and exact-source fingerprints. Explicit Sections and supporting Elements remain available where independently named support is useful; a caption does not create a Section or broaden Element nesting rules. Fenced headings are ignored, and orphan, skipped-level or Check/Element-owned Check declarations are rejected. Legacy accepted grammar is unchanged.

## Stable identity decision superseding qualified normalization: 2026-10-02

Dima approved the coordinated stable entity identity implementation under module-qualified-check-identities (its administrative name records the superseded initial decision). Claim, Case, Mechanism and Check IDs now remain independent of module membership and Case parent relationships. Earlier dated local/qualified normalization decisions and product observations above are historical; they do not describe current accepted parser syntax. Current declarations and references use stable project-wide lower-kebab IDs, source tags identify one stable target, and complete-project validation rejects same-kind duplicate IDs. No aliases or syntax-only alpha protocol version increases are introduced. Ordinary captions and Claim-level Mechanism structure remain valid.
